<div class="grpo-article">
  <section aria-labelledby="what-i-built">
    <h2 id="what-i-built">What I built</h2>
    <p>Can you make a 4-billion-parameter model comparable to a 27-billion-parameter model using RL? RL environments keep coming up in recent model releases. They give models tasks to practice and a way to check their answers. Xiaomi released <a href="https://mimo.mi.com/docs/en-US/news/latest/v2-6">thousands of open RL environments with MiMo-V2.6</a>, and Hugging Face's FineEnvs project includes environments for small models. I wanted to explore one simple question: can you use these open environments on your own hardware to make a small model better?</p>
    <p>I used <a href="https://huggingface.co/datasets/FineEnvs/SmolDataEnvs">SmolDataEnvs</a>, a collection of verifiable data analysis tasks built around real Kaggle datasets. The model gets a file and a question, investigates through a terminal, and saves its answer. I added a local sandbox and a stricter checker, then trained Qwen3.5-4B with rank-64 LoRA adapters on an <strong class="gpu-name">AMD Instinct MI300X</strong>.</p>

<section aria-labelledby="how-it-works">
  <h2 id="how-it-works">How it works</h2>
  <p>
    The original checker could accept "42, yes" when the expected answer was "42." I wrote a
    stricter checker that requires a bare numeric answer for numeric tasks, and used it as the
    training reward.
  </p>
  <p>
    I used TRL's <a href="https://huggingface.co/docs/trl/grpo_trainer">GRPO trainer</a> to update
    the model from the environment's rewards. For each task, the model makes eight attempts. The
    checker rewards a correct answer with 1 and an incorrect answer with 0. Training reinforces
    attempts that beat their group's average reward.
  </p>
  <p>
    Groups where every attempt succeeds or fails offer no relative reward signal. I screened about
    1,000 tasks and kept 240 with mixed success. These gave the model useful opportunities to
    practice.
  </p>
